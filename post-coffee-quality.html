How Xipton Group Is Elevating Uganda’s Coffee Quality

A look at the practices helping our partner farmers grow, harvest and process better coffee.

Quality coffee starts long before it reaches our processing yard. It begins on the farm — with how cherries are picked, how parchment is dried, and how carefully each stage is handled before the beans ever leave the district.

Our field team works closely with partner farmers on the practices that make the biggest difference: picking only ripe cherries, drying parchment evenly on raised beds, and sorting out defects before storage. None of this is complicated, but it does require consistency — and that’s where training comes in.

Training That Sticks

Rather than one-off sessions, we run recurring workshops tied to the coffee calendar — covering harvest timing before the picking season, and drying and storage practices as parchment starts coming in. Farmers see the results in the prices they’re paid, which reinforces the habit season after season.

What Comes Next

We’re continuing to invest in our grading and storage infrastructure so that the quality built on the farm is protected all the way through to dispatch. It’s a long-term commitment, but one that benefits everyone in the chain — farmer, processor and buyer alike.
